Astronomical

Group: Sources. Type: PnAstronomical.

Emits the Sun's position and the day's solar events for a named place, recomputed every period seconds. Type a city and the node resolves it to coordinates once through Open-Meteo's free, key-less geocoder (the only network call — cached until the city changes); every figure after that is computed locally from the NOAA solar-position equations, so it keeps ticking with no further requests. The most common use is the boolean Sun up/down signal — wire data.value into an Edge detector to act on sunrise and sunset, or into a Switch/Filter to gate a flow on daylight.

The datum property (a combobox) picks which computed quantity becomes the canonical data.value. The default, The Sun is up, emits 1.0 when the Sun is above the horizon and 0.0 when below (the standard −0.833° sunrise/sunset horizon, so it agrees with the reported times); The Sun is down is its inverse, handy for gating a flow on darkness without a separate inverter. Whatever the selected datum, every message also carries the full set of fields below, so a downstream node can read any of them regardless of the combobox.

MemberDescription
data.value The quantity chosen by datum — a 1.0/0.0 boolean for The Sun is up / The Sun is down, or the altitude, azimuth, or day length.
data.success true once the city is resolved; false with a reason in data.output while the lookup is unresolved.
data.sun_up Boolean: is the Sun above the horizon right now.
data.sun_altitude Solar altitude in degrees (negative below the horizon).
data.sun_azimuth Solar azimuth in degrees, 0° = north, clockwise.
data.sunrise / data.sunset Times of today's sunrise and sunset as ISO 8601 strings in the location's time zone, e.g. 2026-06-21T04:43:00+02:00. Omitted during polar day/night when the Sun does not cross the horizon.
data.solar_noon Time the Sun is highest, as an ISO 8601 string in the location's time zone.
data.timezone IANA name of the location's time zone, e.g. Europe/Berlin (omitted if the geocoder did not supply one, in which case times fall back to UTC).
data.day_length Hours of daylight today (0 in polar night, 24 in polar day).
data.latitude / data.longitude The resolved coordinates of the city.
data.city / data.country The canonical place name the geocoder returned.
data.output A short summary, e.g. At Berlin the Sun is up; the numeric detail lives in the fields above.
PropertyDescription
city Place to compute for, e.g. Berlin. Geocoded once via Open-Meteo.
datum Which computed quantity drives data.value: The Sun is up (default, 1.0 when up), The Sun is down (1.0 when down), Sun altitude, Sun azimuth, or Day length.
period Seconds between emissions. The default is 60; the position drifts slowly, so a short period is rarely needed.

Sunrise, sunset and solar noon are reported for the UTC calendar day of the current instant, as ISO 8601 timestamps carrying the location's own UTC offset — so the wall-clock part already reads in local time for the place, and a Format node can restyle them as needed.
